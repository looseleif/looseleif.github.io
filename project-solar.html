Electronics & vehicle operation

Circuit boards, cockpit, communications, and the tablet interface.

A complete student-built vehicle

The University of Minnesota Solar Vehicle Project brings together students working across aerodynamics, solar generation, electrical systems, vehicle dynamics, operations, and race strategy. The team designs, builds, tests, and races working vehicles. Its cruiser-class focus also asks how efficiently and practically a car can carry people. About UMNSVP.

Freya was the team's 14th vehicle. The 2022 event program lists a carbon-fiber monocoque, a 1,000-watt solar array, and two in-house brushless motors. Multi-Occupant Vehicle scoring combined energy efficiency with practicality, making usable interfaces and effective vehicle operation part of the broader engineering challenge.

Software, hardware, and the driver's seat

My work from November 2021 through May 2023 included the F1 infotainment application and G1 telemetry hardware, alongside contributions to existing systems and software and hardware architecture. I also drove in the 2022 American Solar Challenge and filmed and developed promotional material for the team.

These roles connected three parts of the same vehicle: the electronics that support operation, the interface used inside it, and the experience of driving it. The engineering value lies in bringing hardware and software into a working system that people can use, test, and maintain under real operating conditions.

The photos and recordings on this page show my electronics, cockpit, and interface work. The news coverage below documents the wider team effort and race context.

Why telemetry matters

Solar racing requires decisions about speed, available energy, and changing conditions throughout a long drive. The crew needs a continuing view of the vehicle's condition to make those decisions. Telemetry connects the car's electrical systems with the people monitoring and supporting it.

Digi's technical case study describes Freya sending vehicle-bus and sensor data through XBee radio modules to a diagnostic computer in the support vehicle. The team monitored speed, battery levels, and energy use, and retained the data for analysis. The radio connection also supported operation along routes with intermittent cellular coverage.

This is the practical context for my infotainment and telemetry work: making vehicle information available to drivers and the team supporting them.

Reliability, testing, and the 2022 result

The university's contemporary coverage describes extensive electrical work during the 2021-2022 school year following the previous race's battery failure. The team's preparation included road testing and resolving faults before the race. Read the university's July 2022 announcement.

The result was a class win for Freya and the Abe Poot Teamwork Award. For this project, that outcome connects engineering with sustained operation: the car, crew, and supporting systems had to work together over consecutive days on the road.
